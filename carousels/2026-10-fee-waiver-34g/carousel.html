First, the basics

A fee waiver is a request. Not an application.

  • You ask the Home Office to waive the extension fee because you can't afford it.
  • If they agree, you then submit your actual extension (FLR) application.
  • Done right, the date you asked for the waiver counts as your application date.
  • That's what keeps you protected if your visa runs out while you wait.

New from 8 October 2026

Same route.
No switching.

Your follow-up application must be on the same route you named in your fee waiver request.

Example: you asked for a waiver on the partner route. Your extension must also be on the partner route.

Apply on a different route (e.g. private life) and your application date becomes the day you actually submit it. The waiver date no longer counts.

Then watch the clock
10
working
days

From the day you receive the fee waiver decision, you have 10 working days to submit your completed application.

  • Weekends and bank holidays don't count. Don't guess, count them.
  • Paid and applied before the decision arrived? Your waiver date still stands, as long as it's the same route.

Miss it, and here's what happens

Your protected date disappears.

1 Nov

Fee waiver request sent. Visa still valid.

20 Nov

Visa expires. You're covered, if you follow up on time.

5 Dec

Fee waiver decision received. 10 working day clock starts.

Too late

You submit after day 10. Your application date is now that day, weeks after your visa ran out.

That gap can leave you without lawful status, putting your right to work, rent and future applications at risk.
